01 · The problem

It started with a missed-call problem.

In care and other service-led businesses, the first phone call can carry a lot of weight. It might be a daughter looking for dementia care for her father, a family arranging respite care, or someone calling after a hospital discharge.

Those calls do not conveniently happen only during working hours. They arrive in the evening, at weekends, on bank holidays, while the receptionist is away from the desk, or when the team is already helping somebody else.

If a human team cannot answer every call, could AI cover the gap without trying to replace the humans?

That question became the starting point for CareHalo.

The gap worth solving

I wanted to explore an AI-assisted layer for a moment that businesses often miss:

  • Calls arriving outside normal working hours
  • Reception teams not always available
  • Missed calls becoming missed opportunities
  • Slow follow-up reducing trust
  • Urgent situations needing faster visibility

05 · Phase 1

Finding the right voice AI.

Why ElevenLabs became the conversational layer

I initially explored Vapi, but I wanted something that felt more natural for a British customer-care style interaction. ElevenLabs gave me a better environment for testing voice quality, prompt behaviour, knowledge grounding and model selection in one place.

British English Customer care Customer support Conversational Natural Calm Clear British voice

Those search terms helped narrow the voice library towards something suitable for a UK support interaction rather than a generic AI voice.

The system prompt

The LLM provides the intelligence. The system prompt provides the role, behaviour and boundaries. I kept refining the instructions based on how the calls actually sounded.

Prompting focused on making CareHalo calm, clear, more human, less robotic and better at listening properly before it responded.

Capture names, phone numbers and email addresses accurately.
Pause naturally so the conversation does not feel machine-like.
Ask clarifying questions without turning the call into a rigid questionnaire.
Recognise urgency and collect the details needed for proper follow-up.

The knowledge base

To test the system safely, I created a fictional care home called Meadowbrook. That let me ground the agent on care-specific information without using real organisations or sensitive live data.

The knowledge base included service information, care types, respite support, CQC-style details, carehome.co.uk-style ratings context and other public-facing information a caller might reasonably ask about.

06 · Phase 2

Bubble: turning a conversation into a usable operational workflow.

Why Bubble mattered

Once the voice layer was working, the next question was obvious: what happens when the call ends? Bubble became the application layer, the database, the dashboard and the orchestration engine behind CareHalo.

I also experimented with Bubble’s AI agent while developing the dashboard. It helped accelerate the starting structure and some of the heavier lifting, but the product thinking still had to come from me — especially around what information should be shown prominently to a manager.

How the conversation summary works

Bubble does not simply store raw output. It becomes the place where the call outcome is shaped into a human-readable summary — something a manager can understand quickly without listening back to the full recording.

Why it matters

That summary is one of the most valuable parts of the product. It turns a phone conversation into something actionable: who called, what they need, how urgent it is and what should happen next.

08 · Before cost

What about UK GDPR and the data?

Once the prototype was working, the next question became essential: if CareHalo is listening to real people discussing care needs, where does all that information actually go?

ElevenLabs

Processes the live conversation and can retain transcripts or audio based on configuration. In a real deployment, retention should be intentionally set rather than left on long defaults.

Voice layer + LLM reasoning
Bubble

Stores the structured enquiry record. Access, privacy rules, retention and deletion workflows should be actively designed rather than assumed.

Application layer + database
SendGrid

Should only receive the minimum information needed to notify the team. Sensitive care details are better kept inside the authenticated dashboard.

Email notification layer
Twilio

Handles telephony for escalation. In a real implementation, call recording should only be enabled where there is a genuine reason to record.

Urgent escalation layer
The simple rule: collect what is needed, store what is needed, keep it only for as long as it is needed, and tell the caller clearly that their information is being processed. For a real UK rollout, organisations would also need appropriate lawful basis, handling for special-category data and a proper DPIA where required.
09 · Cost strategy

10 · What I learned

The project was really about product judgement.

Voice is more than text-to-speech

Pacing, interruption behaviour, listening and tone matter as much as voice quality itself.

Prompting is iterative

The first prompt is never the final one. Real testing reveals what actually sounds human.

Knowledge grounding matters

An agent becomes much more useful when it has safe, relevant information it can refer to.

Bubble made the idea tangible quickly

It let me move from voice experiment to a usable product workflow without a traditional stack.

Conversation summary is the hidden value

Turning speech into a quick, readable manager summary makes the call much easier to act on.

AI works best here as coverage, not replacement

The strongest use case is filling the gap when humans are unavailable — not pushing humans out of the process.

Final outcome

CareHalo turns a phone conversation into an actionable enquiry — captured, understood, prioritised and ready for human action.

This is not presented as a production care system. It is a working prototype and a build story — designed to explore the stack, the workflow, the economics and the role AI can play when people cannot always be there.
