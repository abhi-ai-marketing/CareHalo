01 · The problem

It started with a missed-call problem.

In care and other service-led businesses, the first phone call can carry a lot of weight. It might be a daughter looking for dementia care for her father, a family arranging respite care, or someone calling after a hospital discharge.

Those calls do not conveniently happen only during working hours. They arrive in the evening, at weekends, on bank holidays, while the receptionist is away from the desk, or when the team is already helping somebody else.

If a human team cannot answer every call, could AI cover the gap without trying to replace the humans?

That question became the starting point for CareHalo.

☎

The gap I wanted to explore

  • Calls arriving outside normal working hours
  • Reception teams not always available
  • Missed calls becoming missed opportunities
  • Slow follow-up reducing trust
  • Urgent situations needing faster visibility
02 · Product philosophy

AI when humans are unavailable — not instead of humans.

The model I wanted to explore was deliberately simple: keep people at the centre during normal operations, and use AI as the safety net when nobody can answer immediately.

👥

When the team is available

Humans answer, build trust, understand nuance, make judgement calls and guide families through the next step.

✦

When the team is unavailable

AI listens, captures the enquiry, structures the information, notifies the team and escalates an urgent case if necessary.

04 · The core system

What CareHalo is designed to do.

A caller speaks naturally. The system turns that conversation into a manager-ready enquiry and triggers the right follow-up based on urgency.

05 · Curiosity became a build

I wanted to understand what actually sits underneath an AI receptionist.

I had been exploring products including Lottie, Maxxo AI and other voice-AI receptionist builds. Rather than trying to copy a finished commercial platform, I set myself a smaller, more useful challenge: build a working model and understand the stack.

Voice

What makes one agent sound robotic while another feels natural enough to continue speaking to?

Workflow

Where does the conversation go after the call, and how does a manager turn it into action?

Economics

What does the whole system cost if it handles real out-of-hours call volume?

The system prompt gave the agent its behaviour.

A system prompt acts like the agent's internal operating guide: role, tone, information to collect, boundaries and conversation behaviour.

The prompt was not written once and left alone. Testing exposed little things that made the conversation feel artificial, so I kept refining it.

# CareHalo agent behaviour — redacted reconstruction
ROLE: calm after-hours care enquiry assistant
TONE: natural, patient, clear British customer-care style
LISTEN: allow the caller to finish before replying
CAPTURE: name, phone, email, care need, timeframe, urgency
CONFIRM: contact details where accuracy matters
PACE: use natural pauses; do not sound like a questionnaire
URGENT: recognise escalation signals and flag clearly

07 · Before cost

What happens to the data?

A real caller could share names, contact details and health-related care needs. In a UK production deployment, privacy cannot be an afterthought. Health information can be special-category personal data under UK GDPR.

ElevenLabs

Processes the conversation. Retention should be deliberately configured rather than simply leaving a long default because storage is available.

Design aim: minimise audio/transcript retention.
Bubble

Stores the structured business record. Access controls, privacy rules and a defined deletion/anonymisation policy matter.

Design aim: store the useful record, not unnecessary duplicates.
SendGrid

Only needs enough information to deliver the notification. Detailed sensitive information can remain behind the authenticated dashboard.

Design aim: minimise email content.
Twilio

Used for telephony/escalation. There is no reason to record the manager escalation call unless a genuine business requirement exists.

Design aim: no unnecessary recording.

My UK-GDPR design principles

  • Tell callers when they are interacting with AI
  • Collect only what is genuinely required
  • Keep sensitive detail inside authenticated systems
  • Set explicit retention periods
  • Use Bubble privacy rules and user permissions
  • Review processors, DPAs and international transfers

Before a real rollout

A production organisation should complete the appropriate privacy, information-governance and legal review. Because this concept could involve AI and health-related information, a Data Protection Impact Assessment may be required depending on the exact deployment and risk.

This prototype is different: CareHalo was built using fictional/test information. It is a learning and portfolio build, not a live care-data deployment.

Cost per call is only half the story.

The commercial question is not simply “does the software cost $90?”. The better question is whether it captures calls that would otherwise be lost and helps the team respond faster.

Coverage

How many out-of-hours calls were actually answered?

Quality

How many enquiries were captured with enough information to act?

Speed

Did urgent enquiries reach the right person sooner?

Outcome

Did better handling lead to more meaningful follow-up or placements over time?

AI can do its part perfectly and the workflow can still fail if nobody follows up.

That is why I would test this for 8–12 weeks in one controlled use case, measure the complete journey, and then decide whether it is genuinely adding value.

09 · What I learned

The stack is not the secret. The decisions between the tools are.

Voice is not just text-to-speech

Pacing, listening behaviour, interruption handling and tone change the entire experience.

Prompt engineering is iterative

Real calls exposed issues that were impossible to see by only reading the prompt.

Knowledge grounding matters

The agent becomes useful when it can answer factual questions rather than only hold a generic conversation.

The most expensive LLM is not automatically the best choice

For real-time voice, latency and economics matter alongside raw model capability.

The call is only the beginning

A great conversation followed by a poor operational workflow is still a poor product.

Bubble made the idea tangible quickly

Database, interface, workflows and integrations could be explored without building a traditional full stack.

AI builders accelerate, but product judgement remains

Bubble AI helped with the heavy lifting; deciding what the manager actually needs still required human thinking.

Integration is where the product appears

ElevenLabs, Bubble, SendGrid and Twilio are individual tools. CareHalo appears when they hand work to each other.

Privacy is part of architecture

Retention, access, disclosure and data minimisation are product-design questions, not final legal decoration.

AI is strongest here as coverage

Use it where humans cannot always be present, while keeping human judgement at the centre.
